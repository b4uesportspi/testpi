## 1. POLICY STATEMENT

B4U Esports is committed to preventing money laundering, terrorist financing, and other financial crimes. We maintain a **ZERO TOLERANCE** approach and comply with all applicable Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorist Financing (CTF) laws and regulations globally.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

**Money Laundering:** The process of concealing the origin of illegally obtained money.

**Terrorist Financing:** Providing funds for terrorist acts or to terrorist organizations.

**Suspicious Activity:** Transactions that appear unusual, lack clear purpose, or raise red flags.

**Beneficial Owner:** The natural person who ultimately owns or controls an account or transaction.

---

## 4. PROHIBITED ACTIVITIES

### 4.1 Money Laundering Stages We Monitor

**Placement:** Introducing illegal funds into the financial system
- Large cash deposits followed by immediate withdrawal
- Structuring transactions to avoid thresholds
- Using multiple accounts to layer funds

**Layering:** Concealing the source of funds through complex transactions
- Rapid movement between accounts
- Multiple currency conversions
- Unusual transaction patterns

**Integration:** Making illegal funds appear legitimate
- Purchasing high-value items for resale
- Fake business transactions
- Shell company transactions

### 4.2 Specific Prohibitions

Users are STRICTLY PROHIBITED from:
- Using stolen cryptocurrencies or digital assets
- Laundering proceeds of crime
- Financing terrorism or sanctioned entities
- Evading economic sanctions
- Tax evasion schemes
- Wire fraud or mail fraud
- Bank fraud or securities fraud
- Identity theft or impersonation
- Market manipulation
- Any financial crime under applicable law

---

## 5. CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE (CDD)

### 5.1 Know Your Customer (KYC) Requirements

We collect and verify:
- Full legal name
- Date of birth (must be 18+)
- Residential address
- Government-issued ID
- Contact information
- Source of funds (for large transactions)

### 5.2 Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

For higher-risk situations:
- Large transaction volumes
- Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs)
- High-risk jurisdictions
- Unusual transaction patterns
- Complex ownership structures

Additional verification may include:
- Proof of income
- Bank statements
- Business registration documents
- Source of wealth documentation

### 5.3 Ongoing Monitoring

We continuously monitor:
- Transaction patterns and volumes
- Account activity
- Changes in user information
- Compliance with terms
- Suspicious behavior indicators

---

## 6. TRANSACTION MONITORING

### 6.1 Automated Monitoring Systems

We use automated systems to detect:
- Unusual transaction sizes
- Frequent transactions without clear purpose
- Transactions with sanctioned countries
- Rapid in-and-out fund movements
- Round-trip transactions
- Structuring patterns

### 6.2 Red Flags

Examples of suspicious activity:
- Transactions inconsistent with user profile
- Reluctance to provide required information
- Use of multiple identities or accounts
- Transactions with no economic sense
- Attempts to avoid reporting thresholds
- Pressure to complete transactions quickly
- Involvement of high-risk jurisdictions
- Unexplained third-party payments

### 6.3 Transaction Limits

We may impose limits on:
- Daily transaction amounts
- Monthly transaction volumes
- Number of transactions per period
- Withdrawal amounts

Limits vary based on:
- Account verification level
- Transaction history
- Risk assessment
- Regulatory requirements

---

## 7. REPORTING OBLIGATIONS

### 7.1 Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs)

We file SARs when we detect:
- Potential money laundering
- Terrorist financing
- Fraud or theft
- Sanctions violations
- Other financial crimes

### 7.2 Reporting Thresholds

Mandatory reporting for:
- Transactions above regulatory thresholds
- Suspicious transactions regardless of amount
- Attempted transactions that would require reporting if completed

### 7.3 Cooperation with Authorities

We cooperate fully with:
- Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs)
- Law enforcement agencies
- Regulatory bodies
- International enforcement organizations

---

## 8. SANCTIONS COMPLIANCE

### 8.1 Sanctions Lists Screening

We screen against:
- OFAC (US Treasury) lists
- UN Security Council lists
- EU sanctions lists
- UK HMT lists
- Other international sanctions lists

### 8.2 Prohibited Jurisdictions

We do not conduct business with:
- Sanctioned countries (as designated by UN, US, EU)
- Users in embargoed regions
- Entities owned by sanctioned persons

### 8.3 Blocked Transactions

If a transaction involves a sanctioned party:
- Transaction is blocked immediately
- Funds are frozen
- Relevant authorities are notified
- Account is suspended pending investigation

### 9.1 Required Records

### 9.2 Retention Period

Records are available to:
- Authorized compliance personnel
